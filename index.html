Case 04 · AI prototype · 2026

Zettel: learning German in chunks

It started with my keychain: a ring of word cards I carried through my German learning. Zettel turns that ring into an app. Open a word card, flip it to the phrases the word lives in, keep the ones you’d say, then practice them with fill-the-gap questions.

Role

Concept, UX, UI and working prototype

Built with

Claude

Platform

Web, desktop and mobile

Try it

Open Zettel

Key decisions

Built around how I actually learned

  1. Start from a real object

    The interface copies the ring of paper cards I really used, so it feels familiar before any instructions.

  2. Learn chunks, not single words

    Each word opens to the phrases it lives in, because that is how people actually remember and use a language.

  3. Practice only what you chose

    Practice uses the phrases you kept, and the ones you missed come first next time.

  4. Room for your own phrases

    You can add a phrase of your own, and it gently checks that the word is in it so you can practice it later.

Case 05 · AI prototype · 2026

Birds over me: a diary of the sky

Over the last few years I’ve become fascinated by birds, and I love spotting them. I built this to understand them better. Birds cross a painted sky that follows each city’s real time of day. Tap one to stop it and read its specimen card: its route, its season and where it travels.

Role

Concept, research, UX, UI and working site

Built with

Claude

Data

BirdLife, NABU and local bird groups

Try it

Open the site

Key decisions

A field diary, not a dashboard

  1. An analog feel, close to nature

    Paper slips, a naturalist’s specimen card and painted birds, so it feels like a field diary rather than an app.

  2. Motion carries meaning

    Resident birds stay sharp, while travellers, the migrating birds, pass by blurred.

  3. Real time and real place

    Seven cities from Berlin to İstanbul, each with its own birds and its real time of day.

  4. A reason to come back

    Each traveller you stop gives back one torn page of a diary poem, with sources one tap away.
